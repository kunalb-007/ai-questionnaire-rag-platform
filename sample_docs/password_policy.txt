ACME CORPORATION — INFORMATION SECURITY POLICY
Document: IS-POL-001 — Password and Authentication Policy
Version: 3.2 | Effective Date: 01 January 2025 | Owner: CISO

================================================================================
1. PURPOSE
================================================================================

This policy establishes requirements for password creation, management, and
multi-factor authentication (MFA) across all ACME Corporation systems and
applications. It applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties with
access to ACME systems.

================================================================================
2. PASSWORD REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

2.1 Minimum Length
All user account passwords must be a minimum of 14 characters in length.
Privileged accounts (system administrators, database administrators) must use
passwords of at least 20 characters.

2.2 Complexity Requirements
Passwords must contain at least one character from each of the following
categories:
  - Uppercase letters (A–Z)
  - Lowercase letters (a–z)
  - Numbers (0–9)
  - Special characters (! @ # $ % ^ & * ( ) _ + = [ ] { } | ; : , . < > ?)

2.3 Prohibited Passwords
The following password types are explicitly prohibited:
  - Passwords containing the user's name, username, or employee ID.
  - Sequential characters (e.g., "abc", "123", "qwerty").
  - Passwords that have appeared in known data breach dictionaries.
  - Reuse of the last 12 previously used passwords.

2.4 Password Expiry
Standard user passwords expire every 90 days. Privileged account passwords
expire every 30 days. Service account passwords expire every 180 days and
must be rotated using the approved secrets management platform (HashiCorp Vault).

================================================================================
3. MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION (MFA)
================================================================================

3.1 MFA Mandate
MFA is mandatory for all user accounts without exception, including:
  - All SaaS application logins.
  - VPN and remote access connections.
  - Cloud management consoles (AWS, Azure, GCP).
  - Source code repositories and CI/CD pipelines.

3.2 Approved MFA Methods
The following MFA methods are approved in descending order of preference:
  1. Hardware security keys (FIDO2 / WebAuthn) — required for privileged accounts.
  2. Authenticator applications (Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator).
  3. Push notifications via the approved mobile device management (MDM) platform.

SMS-based one-time passwords (OTP) are NOT approved as a primary MFA method
due to SIM-swapping vulnerabilities. SMS OTP may only be used as a fallback
recovery method when approved by the CISO.

================================================================================
4. ACCOUNT LOCKOUT POLICY
================================================================================

4.1 Lockout Threshold
User accounts are automatically locked after 5 consecutive failed login
attempts within a 15-minute window.

4.2 Lockout Duration
Account lockouts are permanent until reset by the IT Service Desk. Automated
lockout release after a time period is not permitted.

4.3 Privileged Account Lockout
Privileged accounts are locked after 3 consecutive failed attempts and require
manager approval and CISO notification before reset.

================================================================================
5. EXCEPTIONS
================================================================================

Any exceptions to this policy require written approval from the CISO and must
be documented in the Risk Register. Exceptions are reviewed quarterly and
cannot be granted for more than 90 days without renewal.

================================================================================
6. ENFORCEMENT
================================================================================

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including
termination of employment or contract. Critical violations (e.g., sharing
credentials) are treated as security incidents and reported to the Information
Security team immediately.
